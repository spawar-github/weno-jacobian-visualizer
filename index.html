Why this approach works the way it does

The physics forces every choice. During adsorption a sharp concentration and temperature front sweeps the column, and the nonlinear CO₂ isotherm makes it self-sharpen. A naïve high-order scheme rings with oscillations around such a front (overshoots that drive a mole fraction out of [0,1] and break the isotherm); a low-order upwind scheme is stable but smears the front. WENO resolves this: smooth regions blend two stencils for high order, but at a steep front the \(1/(\Delta)^4\) weighting starves the stencil straddling the jump, degrading gracefully — sharp fronts and no oscillations, with no tuning.

The finite-volume framing makes those wall values conservative: adjacent cells share identical face fluxes, so mass/energy are conserved by construction. And the resulting ODE system is stiff — the pressure/velocity coupling and fast adsorption create very fast modes — so an explicit integrator is forced into tiny steps for stability, while an implicit solver takes large stable steps at the price of a Newton solve each step, which needs the Jacobian. WENO's compact stencil makes that Jacobian banded, and a banded solve makes the implicit step cheap enough to run hundreds of cycles. Remove any one piece and the simulation is inaccurate, unstable, or too slow.

Mathematical details

Six coupled dimensionless PDEs per cell are solved by the method of lines (groups from the paper's Supporting Information):

Component mass balance (eq 1):

$$\frac{\partial y_i}{\partial \tau}=\frac{1}{Pe}\frac{T}{P}\frac{\partial}{\partial Z}\!\left(\frac{P}{T}\frac{\partial y_i}{\partial Z}\right)-\frac{T}{P}\frac{\partial}{\partial Z}\!\left(\frac{y_i P}{T}v\right)-\Psi\frac{T}{P}\frac{\partial x_i}{\partial\tau}-\frac{y_i}{P}\frac{\partial P}{\partial\tau}+\frac{y_i}{T}\frac{\partial T}{\partial\tau}$$

Total mass balance → pressure (eq 2), solid LDF (eq 3), bed energy (eq 4), wall energy (eq 5), and Darcy velocity (eq 7):

$$\frac{\partial P}{\partial\tau}=-T\frac{\partial}{\partial Z}\!\left(\frac{P}{T}v\right)-\Psi T\!\sum_i\frac{\partial x_i}{\partial\tau},\qquad \frac{\partial x_i}{\partial\tau}=\alpha_i(x_i^*-x_i),\qquad v=-\,\frac{\kappa}{\mu}\frac{P_0}{v_0 L}\frac{\partial P}{\partial Z}$$

with the dual-site Langmuir isotherm (eq 37) and Arrhenius, temperature-dependent affinities providing two-way thermal coupling.

WENO wall reconstruction (eqs 30–31)

$$\alpha_{0}=\frac{2/3}{(f_{j+1}-f_j+\delta)^4},\;\alpha_{1}=\frac{1/3}{(f_j-f_{j-1}+\delta)^4},\;w_k=\frac{\alpha_k}{\alpha_0+\alpha_1},\quad f_{j+1/2}=w_0\tfrac12(f_j+f_{j+1})+w_1\!\left(\tfrac32 f_j-\tfrac12 f_{j-1}\right)$$

Because the face at \(j+\tfrac12\) reaches back to \(f_{j-1}\), each cell residual depends on cells \(j-2\) to \(j+1\): the Jacobian is banded (lower half-bandwidth 12, upper 11 for 6 fields), not strictly tridiagonal.

The implicit step

With \(\dot{\mathbf u}=\mathbf F(\mathbf u)\), an implicit step solves \(\mathbf r=\mathbf u^{n+1}-\mathbf u^{n}-\Delta t\,\mathbf F(\mathbf u^{n+1})=\mathbf 0\) by Newton:

$$\mathbf J=\mathbf I-\Delta t\,\frac{\partial\mathbf F}{\partial\mathbf u},\qquad \mathbf J\,\Delta\mathbf u=-\mathbf r.$$

BDF2 (the ode15s core) uses \(y^{n+1}-\tfrac43y^n+\tfrac13y^{n-1}=\tfrac23\Delta t\,\mathbf F(y^{n+1})\), giving \(\mathbf J=\mathbf I-\tfrac23\Delta t\,\partial\mathbf F/\partial\mathbf u\). The banded structure lets a banded LU solve \(\mathbf J\Delta\mathbf u=-\mathbf r\) in \(O(M\,b^2)\) instead of \(O(M^3)\) — the same sparsity a real solver exploits via JPattern.

Scope: the dimensionless groups are illustrative (not the exact zeolite-13X set), and these are fixed-step integrators, so the comparison isolates stability, order and Jacobian structure rather than the adaptive step-size control real ode15s/ode23tb add.
