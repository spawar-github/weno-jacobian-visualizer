What the simulation solves

The physics forces every numerical choice. During adsorption a sharp concentration and temperature front sweeps the column, and the nonlinear CO₂ isotherm makes it self-sharpen. A naïve high-order scheme rings around such a front — overshoots drive a mole fraction outside [0,1] and break the isotherm — while a low-order upwind scheme is stable but smears the front badly. WENO resolves this: in smooth regions it blends two stencils for high order, but at a steep front the \(1/\Delta^4\) weighting starves the stencil straddling the jump, degrading gracefully.

The finite-volume framing makes those wall values conservative — adjacent cells share identical face fluxes, so mass and energy are conserved by construction. The resulting ODE system is stiff: with \(\psi \approx 279\) and an N₂ transfer coefficient of order 3, the fastest mode decays at roughly \(10^3\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}\), so an explicit integrator would be capped near \(\Delta\tau \approx 2\times10^{-3}\,\mathrm{s}\) — about ten thousand times smaller than the steps taken here. An implicit solver buys those large steps at the price of a Newton solve, which needs the Jacobian; WENO's compact stencil makes that Jacobian banded, and a banded factorisation makes each step cheap. Remove any one piece and the simulation is inaccurate, unstable, or too slow.

Model equations

Six coupled dimensionless PDEs per cell, solved by the method of lines (paper eqs 1–7):

Component mass balance (eq 1):

$$\frac{\partial y_i}{\partial \tau}=\frac{1}{Pe}\frac{\overline T}{\overline P}\frac{\partial}{\partial Z}\!\left(\frac{\overline P}{\overline T}\frac{\partial y_i}{\partial Z}\right)-\frac{\overline T}{\overline P}\frac{\partial}{\partial Z}\!\left(\frac{y_i \overline P}{\overline T}\overline v\right)-\psi\frac{\overline T}{\overline P}\frac{\partial x_i}{\partial\tau}-\frac{y_i}{\overline P}\frac{\partial \overline P}{\partial\tau}+\frac{y_i}{\overline T}\frac{\partial \overline T}{\partial\tau}$$

Total mass balance (eq 2), LDF solid uptake (eq 3) and Darcy velocity (eq 7):

$$\frac{\partial \overline P}{\partial\tau}=-\overline T\frac{\partial}{\partial Z}\!\left(\frac{\overline P}{\overline T}\overline v\right)-\psi \overline T\!\sum_i\frac{\partial x_i}{\partial\tau}+\frac{\overline P}{\overline T}\frac{\partial \overline T}{\partial \tau},\qquad \frac{\partial x_i}{\partial\tau}=\alpha_i(x_i^*-x_i),\qquad \overline v=\frac{4}{150}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{1-\varepsilon}\right)^{2}r_p^2\frac{P_0}{\mu v_0 L}\left(-\frac{\partial \overline P}{\partial Z}\right)$$

Bed and wall energy balances (eqs 4–5) close the system, with a dual-site Langmuir isotherm (eq 37) whose Arrhenius affinities supply the two-way thermal coupling that produces the roll-up.

Note that eqs 1, 2 and 4 each contain time derivatives of the other variables on the right-hand side. Before the residual can be formed, the \(\partial \overline P/\partial\tau\) and \(\partial \overline T/\partial\tau\) pair is solved exactly as a local 2×2 system in every cell, which is what makes the method-of-lines form explicit in \(\mathbf F(\mathbf u)\).

WENO wall reconstruction (eqs 30–31)

$$\alpha_{0}=\frac{2/3}{(f_{j+1}-f_j+\delta)^4},\;\alpha_{1}=\frac{1/3}{(f_j-f_{j-1}+\delta)^4},\;w_k=\frac{\alpha_k}{\alpha_0+\alpha_1},\quad f_{j+1/2}=w_0\tfrac12(f_j+f_{j+1})+w_1\!\left(\tfrac32 f_j-\tfrac12 f_{j-1}\right)$$

Because the face at \(j+\tfrac12\) reaches back to \(f_{j-1}\), each cell residual depends on cells \(j-2\) through \(j+1\): with six fields per cell the Jacobian carries lower half-bandwidth 12 and upper 11 — banded, but not block-tridiagonal. The first interior face uses the half-cell approximation \(f_1-f_0=2(f_1-f_{0.5})\) (eq 32) to supply the missing ghost value.

The implicit step

With \(\dot{\mathbf u}=\mathbf F(\mathbf u)\), BDF2 solves \(\mathbf u^{n+1}-\tfrac43\mathbf u^{n}+\tfrac13\mathbf u^{n-1}=\tfrac23\Delta\tau\,\mathbf F(\mathbf u^{n+1})\) by damped Newton:

$$\mathbf J=\mathbf I-\tfrac23\Delta\tau\,\frac{\partial\mathbf F}{\partial\mathbf u},\qquad \mathbf J\,\Delta\mathbf u=-\mathbf r,\qquad \mathbf u\leftarrow\mathbf u+\lambda\,\Delta\mathbf u.$$

The line-search factor \(\lambda\) is halved until the residual norm actually falls, which is what keeps Newton alive through the first few steps when the inlet front is forming. The step size then adapts on the iteration count: converge in three or fewer and \(\Delta\tau\) grows, need seven or more and it shrinks. Because WENO's nonlinear weights make \(\partial\mathbf F/\partial\mathbf u\) change abruptly across a front, this adaptivity is not a luxury — it is what the paper's ode23s does too.

How closely this matches the paper

Every qualitative feature of Figure 4a is reproduced: the initial outlet velocity dip to ≈0.89, the sharp CO₂ transition onto a plateau at ≈0.127, the temperature plateau near 1.20, and the second transition where the heat front elutes and the outlet finally reaches the 0.15 feed composition. Transition times land about 2 % later than the ≈520 s and ≈3600 s quoted in the paper's text.

Two honest caveats. First, the reference curve drawn here is digitized by eye from Figure 4a/4b/4c, so treat it as a guide rather than data — the dotted verticals at 520 s and 3600 s are the numbers stated in the paper's own text and are the more reliable comparison. Second, the paper's reference used 2000 volume elements; a browser can afford a few dozen, so the fronts here are broader than the published ones. Raising N narrows them — the transition times and plateau levels are already grid-converged, which is why the validation cards compare those rather than the front width.
