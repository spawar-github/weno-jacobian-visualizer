How a PDE becomes a matrix of numbers

Open a paper on adsorption simulation and you meet six coupled PDEs, a dual-site isotherm, and a page of dimensionless groups. It looks like one indivisible wall of algebra. It isn't. It's five decisions stacked on top of each other, and each one exists to fix a problem created by the one before it.

Work through them in order and you'll see why WENO, the Jacobian and the implicit solver aren't three separate topics — they're one chain of consequences.

Where do the numbers actually live?

Step 1 · discretisation

A PDE makes a statement about every one of infinitely many points in the column. A computer holds a finite list. The finite volume method makes that trade-off explicit and honest: chop the column into N boxes and store one number per box — the average of the solution over that box.

That's the whole idea. Drag N below. The grey curve is the true concentration front; the bars are all the solver ever actually knows about it.

The consequence Coarse grid, and the front becomes a staircase — you lose the very thing you were trying to resolve. Fine grid, and you have more unknowns to solve for. Every remaining decision in this walkthrough is about getting a sharp front without paying for a huge N.

The balance that governs one box

Step 1 · continued

Nothing appears inside a box by magic. Its average can only change because material crosses its two faces, or because something is generated inside it. Written out, for box j, that is the generic shape:

$$\frac{\partial u_j}{\partial \tau}=-\frac{F_{j+1/2}-F_{j-1/2}}{\Delta Z}\;+\;(\text{sources inside the box})$$
A rule you can apply by eye Below are the six balances exactly as the paper discretizes them (its eqs 20–24), one per field this simulation tracks. In every one of them: a term written at j+0.5 or j−0.5 is part of the flux divergence — it needs a face value. A term with no half-index anywhere in it is source — purely local to box j. That's the whole rule. Try it on each equation before reading the caption.
coloured symbol — this equation's own u, wherever it appears flux divergence — has a j±0.5 in it source — no face index anywhere

eq 20 · component mass balance — solves for gas-phase CO₂ fraction y

$$\frac{\partial \textcolor{#185FA5}{y_{i,j}}}{\partial \tau}=\underbrace{\frac{1}{Pe}\frac{\overline T_j}{\overline P_j}\frac{1}{\Delta Z}\!\left[\left(\frac{\overline P}{\overline T}\right)_{\!j+0.5}\!\!\frac{\textcolor{#185FA5}{y_{i,j+1}}-\textcolor{#185FA5}{y_{i,j}}}{\Delta Z}-\left(\frac{\overline P}{\overline T}\right)_{\!j-0.5}\!\!\frac{\textcolor{#185FA5}{y_{i,j}}-\textcolor{#185FA5}{y_{i,j-1}}}{\Delta Z}\right]-\frac{\overline T_j}{\overline P_j}\frac{1}{\Delta Z}\!\left[\left(\textcolor{#185FA5}{y_i}\frac{\overline P}{\overline T}\overline v\right)_{\!j+0.5}\!\!-\left(\textcolor{#185FA5}{y_i}\frac{\overline P}{\overline T}\overline v\right)_{\!j-0.5}\right]}_{\textcolor{#B4530A}{\text{flux divergence}}}\;\underbrace{-\psi\frac{\overline T_j}{\overline P_j}\frac{\partial x_{i,j}}{\partial\tau}-\frac{\textcolor{#185FA5}{y_{i,j}}}{\overline P_j}\frac{\partial \overline P_j}{\partial\tau}+\frac{\textcolor{#185FA5}{y_{i,j}}}{\overline T_j}\frac{\partial \overline T_j}{\partial\tau}}_{\textcolor{#5B4BC4}{\text{source}}}$$

The first bracket is dispersion, the second is convection — both live entirely at j±0.5, so both are flux. The source is CO₂ actually leaving the gas for the solid (xi, a different field, left uncoloured), plus two bookkeeping terms that exist only because y is a mole fraction: a change in pressure or temperature dilutes or concentrates it with no flow at all.

eq 21 · total mass balance — solves for pressure P

$$\frac{\partial \textcolor{#185FA5}{\overline P_j}}{\partial \tau}=\underbrace{-\frac{\overline T_j}{\Delta Z}\!\left[\left(\frac{\textcolor{#185FA5}{\overline P}}{\overline T}\overline v\right)_{\!j+0.5}\!\!-\left(\frac{\textcolor{#185FA5}{\overline P}}{\overline T}\overline v\right)_{\!j-0.5}\right]}_{\textcolor{#B4530A}{\text{flux divergence}}}\;\underbrace{-\,\psi\,\overline T_j\!\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\text{comp}}}\frac{\partial x_{i,j}}{\partial\tau}+\frac{\textcolor{#185FA5}{\overline P_j}}{\overline T_j}\frac{\partial \overline T_j}{\partial\tau}}_{\textcolor{#5B4BC4}{\text{source}}}$$

Pressure has exactly one flux term — the Darcy velocity carrying mass through the face — and a source built from how fast every species is adsorbing, plus the same temperature bookkeeping term seen above.

eq 22 · solid phase balance — solves for solid loading x (one copy per species)

$$\frac{\partial \textcolor{#185FA5}{x_{i,j}}}{\partial \tau}=\underbrace{\alpha_i\!\left(x^{*}_{i,j}-\textcolor{#185FA5}{x_{i,j}}\right)}_{\textcolor{#5B4BC4}{\text{source — no flux term exists}}}$$

Apply the rule and it tells you something real: there is no j±0.5 anywhere, because the solid doesn't flow from box to box — a particle's loading only relaxes toward the local equilibrium x* the isotherm predicts. A field with no transport term at all is still one of the six unknowns; it just has a trivial answer to "where do the numbers live."

eq 23 · column energy balance — solves for bed temperature T

$$\frac{\partial \textcolor{#185FA5}{\overline T_j}}{\partial \tau}=\underbrace{\Omega_{1,j}\frac{1}{\Delta Z}\!\left(\frac{\textcolor{#185FA5}{\overline T_{j+1}}-\textcolor{#185FA5}{\overline T_j}}{\Delta Z}-\frac{\textcolor{#185FA5}{\overline T_j}-\textcolor{#185FA5}{\overline T_{j-1}}}{\Delta Z}\right)-\Omega_{2,j}\frac{1}{\Delta Z}\!\left[(\overline v\overline P)_{j+0.5}-(\overline v\overline P)_{j-0.5}\right]}_{\textcolor{#B4530A}{\text{flux divergence}}}\;\underbrace{-\Omega_{3,j}\textcolor{#185FA5}{\overline T_j}\!\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\text{comp}}}\frac{\partial x_{i,j}}{\partial\tau}+\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\text{comp}}}\!\left(\sigma_{i,j}\frac{\partial x_{i,j}}{\partial\tau}\right)-\Omega_{4,j}(\textcolor{#185FA5}{\overline T_j}-\overline T_{w,j})-\Omega_{2,j}\frac{\partial \overline P_j}{\partial\tau}}_{\textcolor{#5B4BC4}{\text{source}}}$$

Conduction and convection are flux, exactly as before. Everything else is source — and this is where the two-way thermal coupling lives: σi·∂xi/∂τ is heat released as CO₂ adsorbs, and Ω4(T−Tw) is heat leaving for the wall. This is the term that eventually produces the slower thermal front trailing behind the concentration front.

eq 24 · wall energy balance — solves for wall temperature Tw

$$\frac{\partial \textcolor{#185FA5}{\overline T_{w,j}}}{\partial \tau}=\underbrace{\Pi_1\frac{1}{\Delta Z}\!\left(\frac{\textcolor{#185FA5}{\overline T_{w,j+1}}-\textcolor{#185FA5}{\overline T_{w,j}}}{\Delta Z}-\frac{\textcolor{#185FA5}{\overline T_{w,j}}-\textcolor{#185FA5}{\overline T_{w,j-1}}}{\Delta Z}\right)}_{\textcolor{#B4530A}{\text{flux divergence}}}\;\underbrace{+\,\Pi_2(\overline T_j-\textcolor{#185FA5}{\overline T_{w,j}})-\Pi_3(\textcolor{#185FA5}{\overline T_{w,j}}-\overline T_{\text{a}})}_{\textcolor{#5B4BC4}{\text{source}}}$$

Only conduction along the wall is flux. The source is the wall picking up heat from the bed and losing it to the ambient — the two couplings that let a hot front inside the column eventually show up as a temperature you could measure on the outside.

The problem all six create Every one of these is still exact — no approximation has been made yet. But every flux bracket you just spotted asks for a value at a face, evaluated at Z = j±½. You only ever store box averages. Closing that gap is the entire job of step 2 — and it is where WENO comes from.

What value sits at the face?

Step 2 · reconstruction

You need a value at the face between box j and box j+1. You have box averages. So you interpolate — but from which neighbours? The paper offers two candidates (eq 30), and they are the two obvious ones:

The centred guess — ½(u_j + u_{j+1}). Splits the difference between the two boxes sharing the face. Accurate when the solution is smooth.
The upwind-biased guess — 1.5·u_j − 0.5·u_{j−1}. Ignores what's downstream and extrapolates from the side the flow is coming from.

Drag the three bars below. When the data lies on a straight line the two guesses agree and there's no dilemma. Create a jump and watch them split apart — one of them is now reading across a discontinuity, and it produces a face value that overshoots every cell it was built from.

WENO's answer: let the data pick

Step 2 · continued

You can't know in advance where the fronts will be — they move. So you can't hard-code which stencil to trust. WENO's move is to measure how rough each stencil's data looks and give the rough one less say. Here is the paper's eq 30, same colouring rule as step 1: the cell averages you actually have are coloured, and each bracket is labelled with the stencil it came from.

$$\alpha_{0}=\frac{2/3}{\underbrace{(\textcolor{#185FA5}{u_{j+1}}-\textcolor{#185FA5}{u_j}+\delta)^4}_{\textcolor{#378ADD}{\text{centred stencil's jump}}}},\qquad \alpha_{1}=\frac{1/3}{\underbrace{(\textcolor{#185FA5}{u_j}-\textcolor{#185FA5}{u_{j-1}}+\delta)^4}_{\textcolor{#5B4BC4}{\text{upwind stencil's jump}}}},\qquad w_k=\frac{\alpha_k}{\alpha_0+\alpha_1}$$
The rule hiding in the algebra Both stencils' jumps get raised to the fourth power before anything else happens. Whichever stencil sits across the smaller jump produces a small denominator, so its α is large and its normalized weight wk dominates the blend. The rough stencil's jump is large, so its fourth power is enormous, and its α — and therefore its say in the answer — collapses almost as fast as the jump grows. Nothing is compared against a threshold; the arithmetic alone does the deciding.

Where everything is smooth both jumps are similar, the weights relax to their default 2/3 and 1/3, and you recover a high-order blend for free — no tuning, no flags, no "detect the shock" logic. That blend is the paper's eq 31, the actual face value used in the simulation:

$$\textcolor{#185FA5}{u_{j+1/2}}=\underbrace{w_0\left(\tfrac12\textcolor{#185FA5}{u_j}+\tfrac12\textcolor{#185FA5}{u_{j+1}}\right)}_{\textcolor{#378ADD}{\text{centred stencil, weighted}}}+\underbrace{w_1\left(\tfrac32\textcolor{#185FA5}{u_j}-\tfrac12\textcolor{#185FA5}{u_{j-1}}\right)}_{\textcolor{#5B4BC4}{\text{upwind stencil, weighted}}}$$
coloured symbol — the cell averages you actually have, wherever they appear the centred stencil's contribution to the blend the upwind-biased stencil's contribution

Does it actually matter? Watch all three run

Step 2 · the payoff

Same equation, same grid, same time step — only the face reconstruction differs. A smooth bump and a square pulse are carried once around a periodic domain, so the exact answer is simply the initial shape, unchanged.

Upwind uses u_j and nothing else — unconditionally safe, and it smears both shapes into mush. Fixed weights is the same two stencils as WENO but frozen at 2/3 and 1/3 — beautiful on the smooth bump, and it rings badly at the square edges. WENO is that same blend with the weights allowed to react.

revolutions 0.00
exact upwind (1st order) fixed 2/3–1/3 weights WENO (adaptive weights)
Why this matters for adsorption Those wiggles are not cosmetic. An overshoot drives a mole fraction below 0 or above 1, you feed that into a Langmuir isotherm, and you get a negative loading or a divide-by-zero. The simulation doesn't degrade — it dies. WENO is what keeps the physics evaluable.

Now you have an ODE system — why can't you just step forward?

Step 3 · stiffness

Every box now has a rate of change you can compute. Stack them all into one long vector — 6 fields × N boxes — and the PDE has become a plain system of ODEs:

$$\frac{d\textcolor{#185FA5}{\mathbf u}}{d\tau}=\underbrace{\mathbf F(\textcolor{#185FA5}{\mathbf u})}_{\textcolor{#B4530A}{\text{every flux + source term from step 1, for every field and box, packed into one function}}},\qquad \textcolor{#185FA5}{\mathbf u}\in\mathbb R^{M},\quad M=6N$$
u — the same state you met in step 1, now stacked into one vector of length M

This is the method of lines, and it is the moment the problem stops being a PDE and becomes something a standard solver could handle. The obvious next move is to march: u_new = u_old + Δτ·F(u_old). It fails, and not by a small margin.

The reason is that this system contains processes running at wildly different speeds — all at once, in the same vector:

The CO₂ front you actually care about takes thousands of seconds to cross the column. The pressure field re-equilibrates through Darcy's law almost instantly. An explicit step size is held hostage by the fastest process even though you only care about the slowest — that is exactly what stiff means.

The mechanism, on one equation

Step 3 · continued

Strip it to the simplest possible stiff problem, whose true solution just decays smoothly to zero:

$$\frac{dy}{d\tau}=-\lambda y$$

Applying explicit and implicit Euler to this one line produces two update rules with the same shape — a multiplier times last step's value. Everything about the difference between them is in that multiplier:

$$y^{n+1}=\underbrace{(1-\lambda\Delta\tau)}_{\textcolor{#C0392B}{\text{amplification factor — explicit}}}y^{n}\qquad\qquad y^{n+1}=\underbrace{\frac{1}{1+\lambda\Delta\tau}}_{\textcolor{#185FA5}{\text{amplification factor — implicit}}}y^{n}$$
The rule hiding in the algebra A method stays bounded exactly when its amplification factor has magnitude ≤ 1. The explicit factor 1−λΔτ drops below −1 the moment λΔτ passes 2 — that's the wall the slider is about to hit. The implicit factor 1/(1+λΔτ) sits between 0 and 1 for any λΔτ > 0, no matter how large — there is no wall to hit.

Push the slider and watch what each method does as the step gets large relative to the decay rate.

Implicit means you have to solve, not evaluate

Step 4 · Newton

The fix for stiffness is to evaluate the rate at the new time rather than the old one. Colour marks the unknown wherever it appears — same rule as every equation so far:

$$\textcolor{#185FA5}{\mathbf u^{n+1}}=\mathbf u^{n}+\Delta\tau\,\underbrace{\mathbf F(\textcolor{#185FA5}{\mathbf u^{n+1}})}_{\textcolor{#B4530A}{\text{the unknown, buried inside a nonlinear function}}}$$

Look carefully at where the coloured symbol sits. It's on both sides, and on the right it's buried inside F — inside a Langmuir isotherm, inside an Arrhenius exponential, inside WENO's fourth powers. There is no rearranging this. You cannot compute u^{n+1}; you can only hunt for it.

So turn it into a root-finding problem. Define a residual that is zero exactly when you've got the right answer:

$$\mathbf r(\textcolor{#185FA5}{\mathbf u})=\textcolor{#185FA5}{\mathbf u}-\mathbf u^{n}-\Delta\tau\,\mathbf F(\textcolor{#185FA5}{\mathbf u})\;\underbrace{\overset{!}{=}\;\mathbf 0}_{\textcolor{#5B4BC4}{\text{true exactly when }\mathbf u\text{ solves the step}}}$$
coloured symbol — the guess you're refining, wherever it appears

and hand it to Newton's method. Each iteration solves a linear system for a correction, applies it, and re-measures how wrong you still are. Step through it below — the bars are the residual norm on a log scale, so each one dropping is roughly a power of ten of error removed.

The point to take away At a reasonable step size Newton converges quadratically — the number of correct digits roughly doubles each iteration, which is why a handful of iterations is enough even for a badly nonlinear system. Push the step size to "very large" and watch it stop converging altogether: the correction keeps getting damped and the residual never falls. That is not a bug in this demo — it's the reason a real solver monitors convergence and shrinks Δτ and retries when Newton struggles, exactly as the full simulation in step 6 does. And notice the recurring price even when it works: every iteration needs a linear system solved, and that system needs a matrix. Which matrix is the subject of step 5.

The matrix Newton needs — and why it's nearly empty

Step 5 · the banded Jacobian

The linear system Newton solves each iteration is J·Δu = −r, where J holds one entry for every question of the form: if I nudge unknown k, how much does residual i move?

$$\mathbf J=\mathbf I-\Delta\tau\underbrace{\frac{\partial \mathbf F}{\partial \textcolor{#185FA5}{\mathbf u}}}_{\textcolor{#B4530A}{\text{how much every residual reacts to a nudge in every unknown}}},\qquad J_{ik}=\frac{\partial r_i}{\partial \textcolor{#185FA5}{u_k}}$$
uk — the one unknown being nudged; the bracket — every residual's response to that nudge, all at once

With M unknowns that's M² entries. For a real run that number gets absurd fast. But almost every one of them is exactly zero, and step 2 already told you why.

Where the band comes from

Step 5 · continued

Trace the dependency backwards. Box j's balance needs its two face fluxes. The face at j+½ was reconstructed by WENO from boxes j−1, j, j+1. The face at j−½ was reconstructed from boxes j−2, j−1, j. Put those together:

residual of box j  depends only on  boxes j−2, j−1, j, j+1

Six unknowns per box turns that into a band of non-zeros hugging the diagonal — 12 below, 11 above, no matter how large N grows. Not tridiagonal, because WENO reaches back two boxes rather than one. That single fact is what makes the whole scheme affordable:

Two savings fall out of this, and both are large. Factorising a dense matrix costs O(M³); factorising a banded one costs O(M·b²), which is linear in M. And building the matrix by finite differences would seem to need one extra evaluation of F per column — M of them. It doesn't:

Two columns further apart than the bandwidth disturb completely disjoint sets of rows, so they can be nudged in the same evaluation and their effects read off separately without ever getting confused. Columns sharing a colour above are probed together. That turns M evaluations into 25 — for any N.

All five pieces, running together

Step 6 · the real simulation

This is the full model — six coupled PDEs per box, dual-site Langmuir isotherm, Darcy velocity, bed and wall energy — carrying CO₂ into a column of zeolite 13X that starts full of nitrogen. Every piece you just built is doing its job here: cells hold the state, WENO closes the faces, the method of lines makes it an ODE system, Newton solves each implicit step, and the banded Jacobian makes each Newton iteration cheap.

What comes out of the column

Two separate things break through, at very different times — and the gap between them is a physical effect, not a numerical artefact.

column outlet
Watch for Adsorption releases heat, so a thermal wave travels along with the CO₂ — but slower. The CO₂ front arrives first and the outlet jumps to about 0.127, which is below the 0.15 being fed in: the hot bed left behind can't hold as much, so some CO₂ stays in the gas while the rest is still being taken up. Only when the heat front finally leaves, thousands of seconds later, does the outlet climb to the feed value. Two transitions, one cause.
